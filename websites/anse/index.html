Overview

ANSE is a small handbag shop for Abidjan: eight bags, each photographed from the front on plain ground, set in a pastel frame that matches the bag, with the handles rising out of the frame onto the white page. One graphic idea, repeated, is the whole identity.

It is a model shop for Jeere, the commerce platform I am building: the shop is a plain static site, and the cart, the order and the payment go through Jeere. A seller can take the layout, swap in her own bags and sell the same day.

The problem

Small sellers in Côte d’Ivoire take product photos on a phone, on whatever background is at hand. On a shop page those photos never line up, and the shop looks improvised next to big brands. They needed a layout that turns a simple, repeatable photo into something that looks art-directed, and a checkout that takes Wave, Orange Money and MTN MoMo as well as cards.

Who it’s for

Sellers of bags and small leather goods who sell on Instagram and WhatsApp and want a real shop without a designer for every photo.

Their customers, on a phone, who want the price, the colour and the delivery fee before they decide.

Constraints

Product photos that a seller can reproduce herself: front view, plain light ground, soft light.

Delivery priced by commune in Abidjan, and mobile money first.

The demo bags belong to their brands, so the page says it is a demonstration, test orders are not shipped, and anyone reusing the model must replace every image.

Process

Art direction

I started from the photo, not the layout: what a seller can shoot on a phone every time. The rule became the brief at the bottom of the home page (plain ground, front view, handles up, soft light), and the frame does the rest.

Building it

The shop is static HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript, so a seller can host it anywhere. Every product carries a Jeere product id; Jeere Connect fills prices, keeps the cart and places the order. Each bag has its own page, with « Ajouter au panier » and « Commander maintenant ».

Checkout

The order page asks for name, phone and commune, adds the delivery fee for that commune, and offers Wave, Orange Money, MTN MoMo, cash on delivery, or a card through Stripe. The order lands in the seller’s Jeere dashboard.

WHY THIS DECISION

A frame in the bag’s own colour with the handles breaking out of it: one move that makes eight unrelated photos look like one campaign, and that any seller can repeat.

WHY THIS DECISION

A light, clean sans-serif instead of an editorial serif, so the bags carry the colour and the page stays quiet.

WHY THIS DECISION

Plain style properties instead of CSS variables for per-item settings, so the shop stays editable in the Jeere Studio without touching code.

What I designed and built

01 - The shop

Home with the collection, the atelier note and the photo principle; a page per bag with material, colour, carry and closure.

02 - The cart and checkout

Cart drawer, delivery by commune, mobile money, cash on delivery and card payment, all through Jeere.

03 - The photo brief

The four rules a seller follows to make her own photos fit the frame.

04 - Legal and image rights

A page that says whose images these are and that they must be replaced before publishing.

Key deliverables

  • Art direction and photo brief
  • Responsive shop: home, eight product pages, checkout
  • Jeere Connect integration and a Jeere store with real orders
  • Mentions légales and image-rights notice
  • A model sellers can pick in the Jeere dashboard

Outcome

ANSE is live as a demo and is one of the models sellers can choose on Jeere. Orders placed on it are real Jeere test orders, end to end.
