Overview

The Ivory Sukundu sells raw, single-donor hair extensions in three textures, and a half wig that snaps in with combs. The brand wanted a site that felt like a fashion magazine, not a discount storefront: ivory, espresso and a little gold, generous white space, an editorial serif.

I designed and built the site in July 2026 on top of the brand’s Shopify catalogue, and in October moved the store off Shopify entirely: its own catalogue, bag, Stripe checkout, order emails and an owner dashboard. The owner edits texts and pictures in place through Jeere, a tool of mine.

The problem

Hair extensions are bought on trust and on a number: which length, how many bundles, which texture for which look. A standard store template answers none of that. The brand needed a site that teaches the length, recommends a unit, rewards the people who come back, and still checks out in a few taps on a phone.

Who it’s for

Black women buying raw hair for a protective style or an install, mostly on a phone, who compare lengths against their own body and want to know what a half wig really involves.

Returning customers, who the brand wants in a loyalty circle with tiers and a member card.

The owner, who needs to change products, photos, prices, promo codes and copy herself, and to see orders with their timeline.

Constraints

The brand’s own photography and wordmark, and a mood set by the owner: quiet luxury, editorial.

Shopify first, because the catalogue and the orders already lived there; later, leaving Shopify without losing the three existing orders, which were imported encrypted.

Canadian taxes: GST and HST collected through Stripe Tax; prices shown in the visitor’s own currency.

Honest copy. The welcome offer lives in email only, free shipping is promised only when it is true, and the hair is labelled by its real origin.

Process

Research

I started from the brand owner’s brief and references, from how hair brands explain length (most do not), and from the questions customers ask before buying: where does 24 inches land, how many bundles for a full look, straight or curly for my face. Those questions became the length guide and the quiz.

User flows

Discover: hero, the half-wig campaign, the collection. Decide: the find-your-length tool (sixteen lengths from 10 to 40 inches, where each lands on the body, how many bundles build the look, and the note that body wave wears about two inches shorter) and a quiz with two personalities. Buy: product page with length and density pills, bag drawer, Stripe checkout, confirmation, order emails. Belong: the Circle, with tiers, strands and a member card, and an account with order history.

Iterations

The hero was redesigned three times: a cursor-spotlight reveal from black to blonde hair, then the model on a champagne background with the brown wordmark, then rebuilt in 4K with a new model and a warmer backdrop with cast shadow and light fall-off. The theme switched from dark to brown-on-ivory on day one. The member card went through six commits in a day, until it read as an embossed physical card with rounded corners.

The biggest iteration was structural: in October the store left Shopify. Own catalogue, bag and checkout, order emails with templates and a log per order, a dashboard where the owner edits products, photos, promo codes, shipping and the site itself, and sign-in links that last 24 hours.

WHY THIS DECISION

A length guide with a real back-view photo and an animated marker, because the number on a bundle means nothing until you see where it lands, and that question was the one customers asked most.

WHY THIS DECISION

The welcome popup closes only through the X, Decline or sign-up, and the discount stays email-only, so the offer is a reason to join the list rather than a coupon anyone scrapes.

WHY THIS DECISION

Moving off Shopify. The brand paid for a platform it used as a database; owning the store cut the cost, let the owner edit in place, and put the checkout in Stripe where tax and currency were already handled.

WHY THIS DECISION

First-party analytics, because a headless storefront was invisible to the platform’s own numbers and decisions were being made blind.

What I designed and built

01 - The brand site

Hero, campaign, collection, Why Us, Circle, contact and legal pages, with page transitions and reveals on scroll.

02 - Length guide and quiz

Sixteen tappable lengths with body landing points and bundle counts; a quiz that recommends a unit between two hair personalities.

03 - The store

Product pages with length and density pills and a hot pick, a bag drawer, promo codes, a sale countdown with struck-through prices, Judge.me reviews, shop-the-model photos.

04 - Accounts and the Circle

Sign-up, sign-in, password reset by email, order history, loyalty tiers and strands, and a member card.

05 - Checkout and the back office

Stripe Checkout branded to the store with GST and HST, order emails, an owner dashboard with orders, refunds, tracking numbers and timelines, and in-place editing of the live site from Jeere.

Key deliverables

  • Site concept, structure and copy direction
  • Visual system (ivory, espresso, gold; serif display and Montserrat)
  • Framer code components for the hero, product page and account (July), then the full React store
  • Length guide, quiz, Circle loyalty and accounts
  • Stripe checkout with tax, order emails, owner dashboard, Jeere editor
  • SEO, analytics, currency display

Outcome

The store is live and taking orders on its own stack, and the owner runs it from her dashboard.
