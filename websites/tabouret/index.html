Overview

Tabouret is a concept for a neighbourhood restaurant with its own ordering app. Guests order from the same page at the table, for pickup or for delivery, on a phone, a tablet or a computer.

It started from a frosted-glass food app design: soft pastel light, glass cards, plates seen from above that spill out of their cards, and black pill buttons. I turned it into a working app.

The problem

Delivery marketplaces take a cut of every order and list a restaurant next to all of its competitors, while the restaurant’s own site is often a PDF menu and a phone number. Tabouret gives one restaurant an ordering flow as smooth as the big apps, under its own name.

Who it’s for

Guests at the table who scan a QR code and want to order without waving at a server, including people with dietary needs who want to filter, not ask.

Pickup and delivery customers ordering from a phone on the way home, who need to know when it will be ready.

The restaurant owner, who changes the menu, prices, hours, fees and promo codes without a developer.

Constraints

A concept with no backend: orders live in the browser and move through the kitchen on their own, and the app says so.

Opening hours and time slots had to follow the restaurant’s own time zone, not the visitor’s.

Everything the restaurant changes had to live in one settings file, so the concept is realistic to hand over.

Free stock photography from Pexels only, cut out as round plates.

Process

Research

I started from the ordering flows of the big delivery apps, what they do well (speed, options, tracking) and what they take from the restaurant (margin and identity), then from the reference frosted-glass design to see what would survive contact with real menus and real options.

User flow

Browse or search the menu, open a dish, make it your own (options, priced extras with a limit, special instructions, live total), add to cart, choose eat in, pick up or delivery, pay, then follow the order on a progress ring and timeline, and reorder in one tap.

Wireframes and iterations

WHY THIS DECISION

Menu and dish side by side in two glass panels on larger screens, so choosing and customising never leaves the page. On a phone the dish takes over the screen instead.

WHY THIS DECISION

Dietary filters (vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, dairy-free) at the top of the menu, because asking the server is the thing people with allergies dread most.

WHY THIS DECISION

Time slots that only fall inside opening hours and a delivery minimum with a free-delivery threshold, because an ordering app that lets you order the impossible costs the restaurant trust and refunds.

WHY THIS DECISION

A progress ring and a step-by-step timeline after checkout, because the minutes after paying are when people get anxious, and a visible state keeps them from calling.

What I designed and built

01 - A menu that feels like an app

Categories, search, a chef’s pick, favourites and dietary filters for vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free and dairy-free dishes.

02 - Dishes you make your own

Each dish has its own options, from how you like your eggs to priced extras with a limit, plus special instructions and a live total.

03 - Eat in, pick up or delivery

A table number from a QR code, a delivery minimum and a free-delivery threshold, promo codes, tax, and time slots that only fall within opening hours.

04 - Live order tracking

A progress ring, a step-by-step timeline, an estimated time, order history and one-tap reorder.

Key deliverables

  • Concept and ordering flow
  • Visual direction (frosted glass, pastel light, top-down food photography)
  • Layouts for phone, tablet and desktop
  • Light and dark themes
  • Menu, cart, checkout and order tracking
  • Static web app, deployed on Vercel

Outcome
