## 1. The bilingual format (catch in French, keep in English)

Default for every TikTok video:

1. **Hook in French**, on screen and spoken, first 2 seconds. This is what
   stops the Francophone scroll.
2. **Switch line**, one of: « Bon, je vous explique en anglais, les
   sous-titres sont là. » / « Okay, let me explain. »
3. **Body in English**, with **burned-in French captions** (CapCut or Edits
   auto-captions, then translate to French and proofread every line; no
   cut-off words). French-only viewers follow the captions; English viewers
   hear you.
4. **Punchline in French** near the end: « En gros : … » one sentence that
   carries the whole point. French viewers who drifted get the payoff.
5. **CTA in both**, French first.

Caption: first line French, second line English, then a question that invites
a fight in the comments (politely). Hashtags mix French, English and Ivorian
tags, 5 to 8.

The ramp: this week the French parts carry about 40% of the words. Over
October, shrink the French to the hook, the captions and the punchline. Watch
the retention curve in TikTok analytics: if more than 30% drop at the switch
to English, shorten the English section or add a one-line French recap in
the middle.

For the one biggest topic each week, film a fully French version too and post
it as a second video. Two videos on the same topic in two languages is
normal on TikTok and doubles the chance one of them hits.

## 2. Rules for strong-topic videos (so they stay sharp and safe)

- **Show the source** on screen with the green screen effect: the article,
  the petition page, the screenshot, both videos side by side.
- **Separate fact from opinion out loud**: « ça, c'est ce qu'ils ont fait »
  then « ça, c'est mon avis ».
- Companies, brands and public decisions are fair game. **Private people are
  not**: no names, no faces, no addresses, unless it is a public figure
  acting in public.
- Petitions: only real ones (Change.org, MesOpinions, official platforms).
  « Signez si vous êtes d'accord », never a call to harass anyone. Link in
  bio for 48 hours, pinned comment with the petition's exact title so people
  can search it.
- Consumer call-outs (the restaurant kind): show the two pieces of evidence
  side by side, say what you observed, let viewers judge. « D'après ce que
  j'ai vu » costs nothing and protects you.
- Faith topics: talk about the practice and the trend, never about a person.
  Expect debate. Moderate the comments, pin the best one.
- Economy and policy: critique the design or the decision with a source on
  screen; no claims you cannot show.
- Your Instagram photo rules still apply: no text on your face, hair in
  frame, no tight zoom. TikTok's own interface covers the right edge and the
  bottom, so keep text out of the right 130 px, the bottom 350 px and the
  top 150 px.

## 3. Rhythm and timing

- **2 videos a day**: one strong topic, one helpful. A reply-to-comment video
  whenever a comment on the strong topic earns it (that is the engine of a
  commentary account: take the top comment, answer it in 20 to 30 seconds).
- **Posting times** (Toronto is ET; Abidjan is 4 hours ahead; Paris 6 hours
  ahead this week):
  - 7:30 am ET = 11:30 Abidjan, 13:30 Paris
  - 1:30 pm ET = 17:30 Abidjan, 19:30 Paris (evening peak at home)
  - optional 5 pm ET = 21:00 Abidjan, 23:00 Paris
- **Length:** 45 to 90 seconds for a strong topic, 20 to 40 seconds for a
  helpful one, 20 to 30 for a reply video.
- **Morning topic scan, 10 minutes:** TikTok « Pour toi » and the search bar
  trends, Change.org « tendances » and MesOpinions, the Ivorian headlines
  (Abidjan.net, Koaci, Linfodrome), X trends for Côte d'Ivoire and France,
  and your own comments and DMs. Pick the one topic that makes you feel
  something, film it within the hour. A strong topic has a 24 to 48 hour
  window.
- **The funnel:** only the helpful creator-money videos (T2, T6, T10) send
  people to @aissakee. Strong-topic videos stay pure: the handle lives in the
  bio, not in the video.
