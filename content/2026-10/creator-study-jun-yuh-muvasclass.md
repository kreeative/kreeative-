# Creator study: Jun Yuh and Abigail Kwame (15 October 2026)

Kee asked on 15 October for a tool that analyses these two creators' videos
and copies what works into a month of her content. The analysis runs through
**vidIQ**, a connector already linked to this workspace: it lists a creator's
Reels with their numbers and watches a Reel scene by scene. No new MCP server
was needed; to run it again on any creator, use the `creator-study` skill in
`.claude/skills/creator-study/` (or just ask for a creator study).

What it cost: 30 vidIQ credits (two Reel lists at 5, two scene-by-scene
watches at 10). 15 credits were left on Kee's plan, renewing on 7 November.

The month this fed: `month-oct-17-nov-15.md`.

### Watched scene by scene

**Jun Yuh, C8d8FidOZfY (32.9M plays).** No voice, one song, 30 seconds.
2013 portrait with the hard line ("My parents had a nasty divorce…"), a
family photo, 2015 "hard year financially", the tiny apartment, the father
sleeping on the floor, then a jump to 2024 in video: graduation, the family
debt paid, his father flown home, tickets to see his mother, "God is good."
The template: a hardship headline over an old photo (0–3 s), the stakes
(3–6 s), rock bottom in two quick photos (6–11 s), the sacrifice (11–15 s),
then the turn to video and three proofs (15–25 s), the resolution (25–30 s).
Why the first two seconds hold: a big year sets up a transformation, and the
first line is a real wound, not a flex.

**Abigail Kwame, Dec7vvSIlmp (her top Reel this week).** One static side shot
in a café, 9.7 seconds: she writes, looks up, writes, smiles. A long POV line
in the top half, soft R&B, no voice. The caption does the teaching and the
call to action. Why it works: the line takes several seconds to read, so the
clip loops and watch time climbs; the before-and-after in one sentence does
the storytelling.

## What works for them

1. **A real story with a payoff beats tips** (Jun's 32.9M and 3.9M).
2. **A comment keyword turns views into leads** (Jun's 1.7K and 11.7K
   comments; Abigail's 97 on "stay away from my free workshop").
3. **A free event that really teaches, then one offer** (Jun's LAUNCH event,
   Abigail's free masterclass and workshop).
4. **Short, no-talking POV and list Reels** carry Abigail's reach this week:
   her top four by plays include three under 20 seconds.
5. **Two versions of yourself side by side** for values content (Jun, 247K).
6. **Tough love in one line** (Abigail: "helped or rescued", "make fewer
   decisions").
7. **Name the viewer and the season in the first line** ("10 weeks left in
   2026") — but Jun's launch-week versions only reached 545 to 3.9K plays,
   so it works as a hook, not as a whole video.
8. **One set, every day** (Abigail films almost everything at the same desk
   with a drink in front of her): decide once, film many.

## What Kee doesn't copy

- **Their words.** Every hook is rewritten in Kee's voice; nothing is lifted.
- **Their numbers.** Jun's "$3,000,000 in 50 days" and Abigail's "4 to 5
  figures a month" are theirs. Kee says only what her receipts show.
- **The launch-week ad Reels** that reach a few hundred people.
- **Four Reels a day.** One a day plus three ready-made posts a week is what
  fits classes and a 20-hour job; Abigail's volume comes after the system.

## Limits of the data

- Plays on Reels posted the day before are still climbing.
